Teacher Presentation & Viva Q&A Guide

Use this structured walkthrough during your lab demo or oral examination to explain your project step-by-step.

🎙️ Recommended 4-Minute Presentation Script
1
State the Research Problem & Gap
"IoT devices need secure wireless updates over constrained networks like LoRaWAN, BLE, or low-power WiFi. Full firmware transfers waste immense battery and bandwidth. While delta patching reduces transfer size, existing protocols either lack version binding inside the signed payload or require two full digital signatures (one before and one after), overloading microcontrollers. VLCDS solves this with a single 200-byte pre-verify manifest."
2
Demonstrate the 3-Stage Pipeline (Click 'Run Complete Demo')
"In Stage 1 (Pre-Verify), the device receives only the 200-byte manifest. It performs 4 checks: Ed25519 signature, base firmware hash, version binding, and hash-chain link. No delta patch bytes are transferred until this passes. In Stage 2, the delta patch is fetched, its hash verified, and applied in RAM. In Stage 3 (Post-Verify), the reconstructed image is hashed and compared to H(target). Because H(target) was committed in Stage 1, zero second signatures are needed!"
3
Demonstrate the 3 Security Attacks (Click 'Launch Attack' under Attacks)
"We demonstrate 3 real attack vectors:
• Version Mismatch: Attacker sends v2→v3 update to a v1 device. Blocked at Stage 1, Check 2 without downloading any patch data.
• Replay Attack: Attacker replays old v1→v2 manifest. Blocked at Stage 1, Check 4 because device's chain head moved forward.
• Tampered Patch: Attacker flips bytes in the delta stream. Caught in Stage 2 before applying to flash."
4
Show Embedded Feasibility (ESP32-S2 DevKit M1 & Benchmarks)
"We implemented this on the ESP32-S2 microcontroller with pure-Python Ed25519 and SHA-256 in MicroPython. The manifest fits in standard network MTUs (200 bytes), RAM overhead is under 20KB, and post-verify takes only ~0.01 ms."

💡 Top 6 Viva / Oral Exam Questions & Answers
Q1: Why Ed25519 instead of standard RSA-2048 or ECDSA?
Ed25519 (Twisted Edwards curve, RFC 8032) provides 128-bit security level with just 32-byte public keys and 64-byte signatures (vs 256 bytes for RSA-2048). Verification is fast, constant-time (protecting against side-channel timing attacks), and does not require a hardware cryptographic coprocessor or random nonce generation during signing (which caused Sony PS3 ECDSA private key extraction).
Q2: How does VLCDS eliminate the second signature in Stage 3?
In traditional 2-stage verification, the device verifies the incoming update and then must verify another digital signature after installation. VLCDS includes H(target) (the SHA-256 of the new firmware) inside the signed manifest verified in Stage 1. By the second-preimage resistance of SHA-256, if the reconstructed firmware's hash matches H(target), it is mathematically proven to be authentic without another expensive asymmetric signature verification (~0.01 ms vs ~0.33 ms).
Q3: How does the hash-chain prevent replay attacks?
Each manifest commits to H(prev_manifest). At manufacturing, the device initializes its chain head to a known Genesis hash (32 zero bytes). When update v1→v2 succeeds, the device advances its internal chain head to SHA-256(manifest_v1v2). If an attacker later intercepts and replays the v1→v2 manifest, the device's Check 4 compares manifest.h_prev_manifest against its updated chain head and rejects it immediately before any patch is downloaded.
Q4: Why can't we simply sign the delta patch file directly?
Signing the delta patch alone only proves that the patch file was generated by the vendor, but does not bind it to the device's current state. If an attacker sends a valid delta intended for firmware v3 to a device running firmware v1, applying the patch would produce corrupted executable code and brick the device. VLCDS solves this by cryptographically binding H(base_fw) and version_base inside the signed envelope.
Q5: How does this fit into the memory constraints of an ESP32-S2?
The ESP32-S2 has 320 KB of internal SRAM. The VLCDS manifest is only 200 bytes. The pure-Python Ed25519 verification requires under 15 KB of heap memory during computation and frees it immediately. Furthermore, delta patches are reconstructed into a designated buffer or shadow partition rather than loading multiple firmware copies into RAM, keeping peak heap usage well within limits.
Q6: What is the main security advantage of Stage 1 Pre-Verification?
Bandwidth and power conservation. In low-power battery-operated IoT, the radio is the single largest consumer of energy. By rejecting unauthorized, replayed, or mismatched updates after downloading just 200 bytes, the device aborts the connection before wasting megabytes of radio transmission or flash write cycles.
